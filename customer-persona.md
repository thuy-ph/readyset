# Customer persona — Aisha Rahman

*The buyer. Names and casting match the landing page and scenario pack: Aisha buys, Leo uses, Emily reports, Marco fixes.*

## Snapshot

**Aisha Rahman · 43 · Portfolio Facilities Manager, Harbourline Property Services, Melbourne.**
Runs facilities across **12 mixed-use commercial buildings** (target segment: 5–50) with a lean team — one coordinator (Leo Park) per five properties. Fifteen years up from building coordinator, so she's done Leo's job and remembers exactly what it costs. Reports to the head of property operations; measured quarterly.

> "I don't need another platform. I need the requests my team already gets to arrive ready to action."

## Firmographics (who to qualify against)

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Segment | Commercial property / facilities management, mixed-use portfolios |
| Portfolio | 5–50 buildings, multi-tenant, AU (Melbourne/Sydney first) |
| Team | 1 coordinator per ~5 buildings; contracted trades (like Marco) |
| Stack | An entrenched work-order system (Maximo, simPRO, Corrigo…) she will **not** replace, plus email/phone/SMS/tenant portal intake |

## What she's measured on

- **Cost per work order** and contractor callout waste (every "couldn't access the room" revisit is her line item)
- **Time-to-resolution** and tenant satisfaction scores across the portfolio
- **Auditability** — when a job goes wrong, she must show who knew what, when
- Retention of good coordinators — Leo burning out is her single biggest operational risk

## Pains (in her words, quantified)

1. **"My coordinators are data-entry clerks with anxiety."** 38% of requests need at least one clarification round; Leo's median is 2.4 days from report to dispatch-ready — none of it skilled work, all of it chasing a room number, an access window, a contact.
2. **"We pay twice for the same leak."** 17% of requests duplicate an existing job across channels. She finds out when two contractors invoice for one fault.
3. **"Tenants judge the building by the silence."** Emily reports twice because nobody acknowledged the first one. That reaches the building owner, and then it reaches Aisha.
4. **"Every tool wants to replace my system of record."** She's been burned by rip-and-replace platforms. Her data, history, and contractor workflows live in the existing system; anything that threatens it is disqualified on sight.

## Buying triggers

- A duplicate-dispatch invoice or missed-access callout fee lands on her desk in the same month as a tenant complaint
- A coordinator resigns citing workload; replacing them takes a quarter
- An audit or dispute where the intake trail (phone + email + portal) can't be reconstructed

## Decision criteria (in order)

1. **Does not touch the system of record** — integrates in front, removable without loss ("turn us off tomorrow and you lose nothing")
2. **Zero adoption cost for tenants and contractors** — Emily keeps emailing, Marco installs nothing
3. **Human control at every consequential step** — merges suggested not automatic, approval click before any write, emergencies straight to humans
4. **Traceability** — every field shows its source; the record stands up in a dispute
5. Time-to-value — a pilot she can run on one building's inbox in a week, not a six-month integration

## Objections she'll raise (and the answers the product already gives)

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "AI will merge two real issues into one and we'll miss a fault." | Duplicates are flagged with reasoning, never merged. Leo confirms. Nothing is deleted either way. |
| "What happens when someone reports a gas leak to a robot?" | Hazard keywords route straight to the on-call human, unprocessed. Readyset only spots them fast. |
| "Legal will ask who wrote what into our records." | Every value carries provenance — tenant-stated, register-matched, or coordinator-entered — and every write is a named human's approval click. |
| "My contractors won't adopt anything." | Marco receives a complete work order in the system he already uses. He never sees Readyset. |

## What winning looks like for her

Time-to-Ready on piloted buildings drops from days to minutes; duplicate dispatches go to zero on flagged pairs; Leo covers more buildings without more hours; tenant acknowledgment is instant and automatic. She presents one number upward: **2.4 days → 4 minutes.**

## Where to find her

FMA (Facility Management Association of Australia) events and its FM magazine; Property Council briefings; LinkedIn groups for AU facilities managers; referrals from coordinators — **Leo-shaped users are the loudest champions**, so demos that make the coordinator's day visibly better recruit the buyer.

## Secondary sketch — Leo Park, the daily user

**Building coordinator, five properties, seven years in.** Not the buyer, but the pilot lives or dies on him. Spends his morning triaging four channels; his stated fear is approving something wrong, which is why the approve-click design (he stays the decision-maker, with a complete record in front of him) converts him rather than threatens him. Success for Leo: the queue arrives triaged, complete, deduplicated — and the 2.4 days of chasing becomes his time back. Win Leo in the demo, and he sells Aisha internally.
